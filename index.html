Hello, I’m Ameya.

I’m a sixth-year Integrated PhD scholar in physics at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Mumbai.

With a background in mechanical engineering, I now work in laser–plasma physics, using high-power lasers to generate and study dense plasmas in the laboratory.

What I do

My research involves conducting experiments that recreate aspects of the extreme plasma conditions found in stars and other astrophysical environments. In the lab, I use intense, ultrashort laser pulses to explore how light interacts with matter and how particles, fields, and shock waves emerge from these interactions.

I combine experimental measurements with particle-in-cell simulations to understand the underlying physics. My work includes electron acceleration, magnetic-field generation, shock-wave dynamics, and the development of diagnostic systems.

Experiments & diagnostics

High-intensity femtosecond laser experiments, pump–probe measurements, Stokes polarimetry, Doppler-shift measurements, and wavefront sensing.

Simulation & analysis

Particle-in-cell modelling with SMILEI and EPOCH, alongside data analysis using Python, MATLAB, and Origin.

Research work

Electron acceleration

I study direct laser acceleration in longitudinal and transverse electric fields. Using two-dimensional SMILEI simulations on the TIFR HPC cluster, I developed a framework and proposed a practical multi-beam electron-acceleration scheme.

Read the preprint ↗

High-intensity laser experiments

At the 1 PW laser facility at ELI-NP, Romania, I contributed to experiments investigating directional MeV gamma-ray emission alongside electron, proton, and neutron emission. I have also contributed to experimental chambers and diagnostics for the new petawatt facility in Hyderabad.

Magnetic fields, shocks & plasma diagnostics

My work includes shock-wave studies with high-contrast 400 nm laser pulses and measurements of laser absorption under varying pulse energy, incidence angle, and prepulse conditions. I have contributed to Stokes polarimetry for megagauss magnetic fields and pump–probe investigations of plasma dynamics.
